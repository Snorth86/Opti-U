# Governed adaptive intelligence

Governance is not a wrapper placed around the model after it works. It defines what “works” means.

## 1. One canonical authority for each fact

State should have one owner. Other surfaces may read governed views; they should not create competing truths.

## 2. Consent is checked at use, not only collection

Permission to collect a signal is not permanent permission to use it for every future purpose.

## 3. Influence must be earned

An output can begin as observation or explanation. Increasing its effect on a user's experience requires stronger evidence, explicit review and a rollback path.

## 4. Population evidence advises; it does not overwrite

What appears to work for a cohort may not transfer to an individual. Population learning remains advisory until relevance, uncertainty and negative-transfer risk are addressed.

## 5. Language has no hidden authority

Generative language may make structured results understandable. It may not convert uncertainty into certainty, association into causation or an unauthorised output into advice.

## 6. Every important decision needs lineage

The system should be able to reconstruct the information, versions, permissions, evidence and fallbacks behind a decision.

## 7. Refusal is a successful outcome

`INCONCLUSIVE`, “not enough data” and safe fallback are not product failures. They are evidence that the system respects its own boundary.

## 8. Rollback is part of the design

If influence cannot be withdrawn quickly and traced afterwards, it should not be released.

## 9. Claims expire

Test counts, readiness statements and performance results are revision-bound. Every claim should carry scope, environment, date and limitation.

## 10. Non-clinical means non-clinical

Opti-U supports everyday behaviour change. It does not diagnose, treat or replace professional care.
